## Why This Matters

Think of GitHub like the **central nervous system of your AI business.**

Every tool you build, every system you design, every blueprint you create — it lives here. Version controlled, organized, protected, and ready to grow with you.

A **GitHub Organization** is your business's home on GitHub. Instead of projects scattered under your personal account, everything lives under your brand:

```
your-business-name/
├── project-one
├── project-two
├── docs-and-playbooks
└── ...
```

This matters because:
- **It looks intentional** — you're building a business, not a hobby
- **It scales** — add team members, collaborators, or contractors later without reorganizing
- **It separates business from personal** — your business projects aren't mixed with everything else
- **It's free** — GitHub Organizations are free for personal and small team use

## Before You Start

You'll need:
- [ ] A GitHub account (free at github.com)
- [ ] Your business name decided — this becomes your org handle (choose carefully, it's hard to change)
- [ ] 10 minutes of uninterrupted focus

**Choosing your org name:**
- Use your business name or a close variation
- Lowercase, hyphens instead of spaces (`sage-ai-studios` not `Sage AI Studios`)
- Check availability as you type — GitHub will tell you if it's taken
- This will appear in all your repo URLs: `github.com/your-org-name/project`

---

## Step 1: Create Your Organization

1. Go to **github.com** and sign in
2. Click your **profile picture** (top right corner)
3. Select **"Your organizations"** from the dropdown
4. Click the green **"New organization"** button
5. On the plan selection screen, choose **"Create a free organization"**

---

## Step 2: Name Your Organization

1. Enter your **organization account name**
   - Example: `sage-ai-studios`
   - This is permanent and appears in all your URLs — choose intentionally
2. Enter your **contact email**
3. Select **"My personal account"** (not a business/enterprise option)
4. Complete the verification puzzle
5. Click **"Next"**

---

## Step 3: Skip the Team Invite (For Now)

GitHub will ask you to invite team members.

- Click **"Complete setup"** — you can add people later
- You're building solo first. That's the right move.

---

## Step 4: Customize Your Organization Profile

This is your business's GitHub homepage. Make it intentional.

1. Go to your org page: `github.com/your-org-name`
2. Click **"Edit organization profile"** (or go to Settings)
3. Fill in:
   - **Description** — one clear sentence about what you're building
   - **URL** — your website if you have one
   - **Profile picture/logo** — upload your brand image
4. Save

---

## Step 5: Create Your First Repository

A repository (repo) is one project, one system, one piece of your business.

1. From your org page, click **"New repository"** (green button)
2. Fill in:
   - **Owner:** Select your organization name (not your personal account)
   - **Repository name:** Start with `docs` or your first project name
   - **Description:** One sentence about what this repo contains
   - **Visibility:** Private (recommended while building)
   - **Initialize:** Check "Add a README file"
3. Click **"Create repository"**

---

## Step 6: Plan Your Repository Structure

Don't create everything at once. Start with what you're actually building now.

A good starting structure for an AI-native business:

```
your-org/
├── docs                 ← Design documents, playbooks, session notes
├── [project-name]       ← Your first actual build
└── mac-setup            ← Your machine configuration (if relevant)
```

**Rule of thumb:** One repo per distinct project or system. When in doubt, start with `docs` and let the structure grow from your actual work.

---

## Step 7: Set Repository Permissions (Optional but Smart)

For a private org with just you right now:

1. Go to **Organization Settings → Member privileges**
2. Set base permissions to **"No permission"** — everything is opt-in
3. This protects you when you eventually add collaborators

## Common Questions

**"Do I need to pay for GitHub?"**
No. The free tier is more than enough to build an entire AI-native business. You'd only need paid tiers if you need advanced team features or enterprise security.

**"What if I choose the wrong org name?"**
GitHub org names can be changed but it breaks all existing URLs. Choose carefully upfront. When in doubt, use your business name exactly.

**"Should my repos be public or private?"**
Private while building. You control what becomes public and when. Nothing wrong with building behind closed doors first.

**"What's the difference between a repo and a folder?"**
A folder is just storage. A repo is living, versioned history — every change tracked, every version recoverable, every decision documented. It's the difference between saving a Word doc and having a full record of every edit ever made.

**"I'm not a developer. Is GitHub for me?"**
Yes. GitHub started as a developer tool but it's become the standard for anyone building systems — AI-native businesses especially. Your playbooks, your AI prompts, your system designs, your blueprints all belong here. Code is just one of many things it holds.
